GETTING STARTED WITH THERAPY AT PSYLING

Starting therapy is a courageous and positive step toward better mental health and wellbeing. This guide will help you understand what to expect and how to begin at Psyling.

========================================
IS THERAPY RIGHT FOR ME?
========================================

People seek therapy for many different reasons. You don't need to be in crisis to benefit from therapy. Consider therapy if:

You're Experiencing:
- Persistent sadness, anxiety, or stress
- Difficulty managing emotions
- Relationship challenges
- Major life transitions
- Grief or loss
- Trauma or past experiences affecting current life
- Feeling stuck or unfulfilled
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Changes in sleep, appetite, or energy levels

You Want To:
- Develop better coping skills
- Improve relationships
- Work through past experiences
- Increase self-awareness
- Make important life decisions
- Break unhelpful patterns
- Achieve personal growth
- Build resilience

========================================
WHAT TO EXPECT IN YOUR FIRST SESSION
========================================

The initial session is all about getting to know you and understanding how we can help.

What We'll Cover:
- What brought you to therapy
- Your current concerns and challenges
- Relevant history (family, relationships, work, health)
- Your goals for therapy
- Questions about the therapy process
- Discussion of confidentiality and your rights
- Treatment approach and what to expect

What You Should Know:
- It's normal to feel nervous - most people do
- There's no pressure to share everything in the first session
- The therapist is there to listen and understand, not to judge
- You can ask any questions about the process
- You're in control of what you share
- It's okay to take your time

Assessing Fit:
- The first session helps both you and the therapist determine if it's a good match
- Not every therapist is right for every client
- If it doesn't feel like the right fit, we can help you find someone else
- Good therapeutic relationship is essential for effective therapy

========================================
PREPARING FOR YOUR FIRST SESSION
========================================

While you don't need to prepare extensively, these tips can help:

Things to Think About:
- What are you hoping to get from therapy?
- What are your main concerns right now?
- Are there specific goals you'd like to work toward?
- What has helped you cope in the past?

Things to Bring:
- Insurance card (if using insurance)
- Payment method
- List of current medications (if any)
- Any questions you have about the process

Things to Remember:
- There are no "wrong" answers
- You don't need to have everything figured out
- It's okay to be emotional
- The therapist is there to support you

========================================
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER THE FIRST SESSION
========================================

After your initial consultation, you and your therapist will decide on next steps together.

Creating a Treatment Plan:
- Discuss how frequently to meet (usually weekly at first)
- Identify specific goals
- Outline therapeutic approach
- Set expectations for therapy

Typical Therapy Schedule:
- Most clients start with weekly 50-minute sessions
- Some situations benefit from twice weekly sessions
- Frequency may decrease as you progress
- Flexible based on your needs and goals

Between Sessions:
- You may be given "homework" or practice exercises
- Reflect on what you discussed in session
- Notice patterns or situations related to your goals
- Reach out if urgent concerns arise (therapist will provide guidance on how)

========================================
CONFIDENTIALITY AND YOUR RIGHTS
========================================

Your privacy is protected by law and by our professional ethics.

What's Confidential:
- Everything you discuss in therapy is confidential
- We don't share information without your written consent
- Protected by HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

Exceptions to Confidentiality (Required by Law):
- If you're at risk of harming yourself
- If you're at risk of harming someone else
- If there's suspected abuse or neglect of a child, elderly person, or dependent adult
- If a court orders release of information (rare)

Your Rights as a Client:
- Right to ask questions about your treatment
- Right to access your treatment records
- Right to end therapy at any time
- Right to a second opinion
- Right to file a complaint if you feel your rights were violated

COMMON CONCERNS ABOUT STARTING THERAPY
========================================

"I'm not sure I'm 'bad enough' to need therapy"
Therapy isn't just for crisis situations. It's a tool for growth, prevention, and wellbeing. You don't need to wait until things are unbearable to seek support.

"What if I don't know what to say?"
Your therapist will guide the conversation with questions. Many people feel this way at first, and it gets easier as you build rapport.

"I'm worried about being judged"
Therapists are trained to be non-judgmental and compassionate. They've heard a wide range of human experiences and are there to support, not judge.

"What if I cry?"
Crying in therapy is completely normal and okay. It's a safe space for all emotions.

"I've tried therapy before and it didn't work"
Every therapeutic relationship is different. A different therapist, approach, or timing can make a significant difference.

"I don't have time"
Therapy is an investment in yourself. Many people find that addressing challenges actually saves time by improving functioning and reducing distress.

"I can't afford it"
We offer sliding scale options, accept various insurance plans, and can discuss financial assistance resources. Please ask about options.

WHEN TO SEEK IMMEDIATE HELP
========================================

If you're experiencing a mental health emergency, please seek immediate help:

Crisis Situations:
- Thoughts of harming yourself or others
- Severe panic or overwhelming distress
- Psychotic symptoms (hearing voices, severe confusion)
- Substance use crisis

Immediate Resources:
- National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
- Emergency Services: 911
- Go to your nearest emergency room

Non-Emergency Support:
- Call our office to schedule an urgent appointment
- SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (24/7, free, confidential)

QUESTIONS TO ASK YOUR THERAPIST
========================================

It's important to feel comfortable with your therapist. Here are some questions you might want to ask:

About Their Background:
- What is your training and experience?
- Do you have experience treating [specific concern]?
- What therapeutic approaches do you use?
- How long have you been practicing?

About The Process:
- How often do you recommend we meet?
- What can I expect from our sessions?
- How will we measure progress?
- How long does therapy typically take?
- What should I do if I need support between sessions?

About Logistics:
- What's your cancellation policy?
- How do you handle after-hours emergencies?
- Do you accept my insurance?
- Can we do telehealth sessions if needed?

========================================
MAKING THE MOST OF THERAPY
========================================

Therapy is most effective when you're an active participant:

Tips for Success:
- Be honest and open with your therapist
- Attend sessions consistently
- Practice skills between sessions
- Communicate if something isn't working
- Be patient with the process
- Ask questions when you're confused
- Share feedback about what's helpful or not

Remember:
- Change takes time
- Progress isn't always linear
- Some sessions will feel more productive than others
- The therapeutic relationship builds over time
- You're in control of your treatment
